### 5. CAMERA MOVEMENT

Use one main camera movement throughout the 10-second shot.

Select the movement based on the garden layout.

**For a garden with a water channel:**
Move slowly forward along the channel, keeping the water in the lower foreground while revealing the vegetable beds on both sides.

**For a bamboo trellis garden:**
Glide gently beneath or alongside the trellis, allowing hanging gourds or beans to become prominent in the frame.

**For a chili or tomato garden:**
Move slowly between the plants at approximately waist or chest height, revealing clusters of colorful vegetables.

**For a papaya-and-vegetable garden:**
Begin with the vegetable beds and gradually reveal the papaya trees and their fruit in the background.

**For a hydroponic garden:**
Move along the PVC growing pipes, showcasing the leafy vegetables in the foreground and the climbing plants behind them.

**For a dense tropical garden:**
Use a gentle forward walkthrough that reveals successive layers of plants and creates a strong sense of depth.

The camera should move at a natural walking pace or slightly slower.

Include subtle handheld movement, but keep the footage stable and comfortable to watch.

Do not use dramatic drone movements, rapid zooms, extreme rotations, or sudden perspective changes unless specifically requested.

**7.0–10.0 seconds: Satisfying final composition**
